## Section PG-POL-1: Roadside Issuance of Spot-Fine Notices (Section 215A & Form 33A)
### PG-POL-1.1: Statutory Basis for Spot Fines
Under Section 215A of the Motor Traffic Act (Cap. 203), when a police officer detects a driver committing a scheduled traffic offence listed in the Second Schedule (as amended by Gazette Extraordinary No. 2054/9 of 15 January 2018), the driver may opt to settle the offence administratively by paying the prescribed spot fine rather than facing immediate prosecution in a Magistrate's Court.

### PG-POL-1.2: Temporary Retention of Driving Licence and Issuance of Notice
1. When issuing a spot-fine notice (Police Form 33A / e-Traffic Notice) or a Court Summons Notice at the roadside, the police officer is empowered under Section 135 and Section 215A of the Motor Traffic Act to take temporary custody of the driver's physical Driving Licence.
2. In exchange for the Driving Licence, the police officer must issue the official written Spot-Fine Notice or Temporary Driving Permit slip to the driver.
3. This official notice serves as a temporary authorization to drive for a strictly limited grace period of **fourteen (14) days** from the date of issuance, subject to any restrictions noted on the slip.
4. Police officers are strictly prohibited from collecting cash payments for traffic fines directly at the roadside.

## Section PG-POL-2: Standard Procedure for Spot-Fine Payment (Within 14 Days)
### PG-POL-2.1: Statutory 14-Day Payment Window
A driver issued a spot-fine notice has **fourteen (14) calendar days** from the date of the offence to pay the prescribed fine amount. If the fine is paid in full within this 14-day window, no court appearance is required and no conviction is recorded in a Magistrate's Court.

### PG-POL-2.2: Method A — Payment at Any Sri Lanka Post Office or Sub-Post Office
1. Present the original Spot-Fine Notice issued by the police officer at any Post Office or Sub-Post Office island-wide before the 14-day deadline expires (selected main post offices, including GPO Colombo, Kandy, Kurunegala, Matara, and Anuradhapura, operate extended hours from 07:00 AM to 08:00 PM / 09:00 PM for fine collection).
2. Pay the exact rupee amount specified on the notice (derived from Gazette Extraordinary No. 2054/9, plus any applicable postal handling fee).
3. The Post Office counter officer will stamp the notice and issue an official **Postal Payment Receipt**.
4. Present the stamped Spot-Fine Notice and the original Postal Payment Receipt to the Traffic Branch of the issuing Police Station (as named on the ticket) to reclaim the physical Driving Licence. If the driver cannot visit the issuing station locally, the receipt can be submitted via registered post or handled through the designated postal license-return service where applicable.

### PG-POL-2.3: Method B — Online Payment via GovPay / Mobile Banking Apps
1. Open an official Sri Lankan commercial bank mobile application or internet banking portal integrated with the government **GovPay** platform (e.g., People's Pay / People's Wave, Bank of Ceylon, Commercial Bank Digital, Sampath Vishwa, HNB Digital).
2. Navigate to **Bill Payments** or **Government Services** and select **GovPay** or **Sri Lanka Police Traffic Fines**.
3. Enter the driver's **National Identity Card (NIC) number** and the **Fine Reference / Case Number** printed on the traffic fine notice to retrieve the offence details.
4. Verify the displayed offence category and fine amount (LKR), then authorize the electronic payment.
5. Save and download the generated digital payment confirmation receipt. Present the digital receipt reference and the original notice at the issuing Police Station Traffic Branch (or follow the e-Traffic verification procedure) to retrieve the held Driving Licence.

## Section PG-POL-3: Procedure for Non-Spot-Fine (Court-Mandated) Offences
### PG-POL-3.1: Offences Ineligible for Spot Fines
Serious traffic offences—including driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs (Section 151), driving without a valid driving licence (Section 123 / Act No. 10 of 2019), permitting an unlicensed person to drive, driving without valid third-party insurance, reckless or dangerous driving causing injury, and railway-crossing violations—cannot be settled via a post office spot fine.

### PG-POL-3.2: Mandatory Magistrate's Court Appearance
1. For court-mandated offences, the police officer retains the driving licence (where applicable) and issues a notice specifying the **Magistrate's Court** jurisdiction and the **date of appearance**.
2. The driver must appear in person at the designated Magistrate's Court on the stated date to enter a plea (Guilty or Not Guilty) before the Magistrate.

## Section PG-POL-4: Procedure for Contesting or Appealing a Traffic Fine or Charge
### PG-POL-4.1: Right to Contest a Roadside Spot Fine
A citizen is under no legal compulsion to accept a roadside spot fine if they dispute the allegation (for example, contesting an uncalibrated exhaust noise/decibel allegation, disputing a speed limit reading, or challenging an improper road-rule charge).

### PG-POL-4.2: Step-by-Step Procedure to Dispute a Spot Fine in the Magistrate's Court
1. **Do Not Pay the Spot Fine:** Paying the spot fine at a Post Office or via GovPay constitutes an administrative settlement of the offence and waives the right to contest the ticket in court.
2. **Request Court Referral / Allow the 14-Day Window to Lapse for Court Filing:** Inform the issuing officer or the Traffic OIC (Officer-in-Charge) at the relevant Police Station that you intend to contest the charge, or withhold payment during the 14-day settlement period. Upon non-payment after 14 days, the Police Traffic Branch is legally required under Section 215A to file a formal plaint in the local **Magistrate's Court** having jurisdiction over the location of the offence.
3. **Attend the Magistrate's Court on the Summons Date:** Appear at the specified Magistrate's Court on the court date noted on the reverse/bottom of the police notice (or the subsequent court summons).
4. **Enter a Plea of "Not Guilty":** When the case number and name are called before the Magistrate, state clearly that you plead **Not Guilty** (either in person or through an Attorney-at-Law).
5. **Extension of Temporary Driving Authorization:** Upon a plea of Not Guilty, the Magistrate will schedule a trial date and may direct the court registrar to endorse or extend the temporary driving permit slip until the conclusion of the inquiry.
6. **Trial and Evidence Presentation:** At the trial date, the prosecution (Police) bears the burden of proving the elements of the offence beyond reasonable doubt (e.g., producing certified decibel meter calibration records under Gazette No. 1738/37 or 924/12, radar speed logs under Gazette No. 1775/32, or vehicle examiner reports). The driver (or counsel) may cross-examine the police officers and present supporting documentary or technical evidence (such as a valid DMT Certificate of Fitness or emission certificate).
7. **Acquittal or Conviction & High Court Appeal:** If the Magistrate finds the charge unproven, the driver is acquitted without penalty and the physical Driving Licence is returned immediately by court order. If convicted after trial, the driver retains the statutory right to appeal the Magistrate's conviction or sentence to the **Provincial High Court** within **fourteen (14) days** of the judgment under the Code of Criminal Procedure Act, No. 15 of 1979.
